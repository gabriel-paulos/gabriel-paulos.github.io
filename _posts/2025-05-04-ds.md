Distributed Agents and what it means for foundational models?

Minions paper: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15964

Google A2A: https://github.com/google/A2A

A problem with the centralization of the largest models on the cloud infra of some of the largest companies in the world, is that the data sent to these models may be 
highly sensitive and there is risk of these models having access to information it should otherwise not have. An obvious workaround would be to create your own model, either
by training one from scratch or fine-tuning an open-source model locally (using Ollama for example). A problem with this solution is that this requires an immense amount 
of resource allocation to build, host and maintain these models. Many companies would not be willing to take on such a proposition. 

Luckily, a better solution has arrived: using a distributed systems of local AI agents that connect to a cloud foundational model. There has been siginificant work in this area (instead for dealing with environments where the context is significantly longer than the windows of any single LLM). Distributed systems concepts have appeared (such as the LLMxMapReduce paper), but there has yet to be a full deep dive of AI agents in distributed systems.  The effectiveness of the solution was recently demonstrated by the Minions paper. 


Most exciting news from the last couple of months was the release of the Minions paper (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15964) and the A2A protocol by Google (and to a lesser degree MCP by Anthropic or Evergreen by Google). The push towards a distributed systems flavour in AI developments and breakthroughs is highly exciting for me as I find distributed systems and its problem space highly fascinating. The Minions paper was a massive breakthrough as it proved that distributed AI agents could work in a highly efficient and effective manner. The introduction of the A2A model would allow local models to collaborate on problems with large foundational models only found at the largest tech companies in a manner that would be more conductive towards the capabilities of the local models; improving performance. I am currently tinkering with this idea, and here is a link to an article I am working on in relation to this news. 

Two things come to mind, however, I shall group them together because they fit into a larger picture (from my perspective). They are: Google A2A protocol (to a lesser extent MCP) and the release of a Minions paper (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15964). In my mind the larger picture is that distributed, federated AI agent networks are the future of the AI paradigm. The reason for this is simple, currently AI adoption is bottlenecked because the best models are controlled by a handful of companies, and these models have poor data control limits (that is there is a chance sensitive data is absorbed into the training data of those foundational models). In order to compromise between performance and some semblance of data control, there is a requirement for entities (companies or people) to have there own local models, but to achieve a certain threshold of performance (especially in reasoning tasks) these entities must have the ability to communicate with the larger foundation models. This problem is set up as a distributed systems problem, while both annoucements are steps towards a common distributed systems solution (a communication protocol). This is a really exciting direction (for me) as the distributed systems space is rich with interesting problems. It is even more interesting with respect to how to communicate modalities other than text, for example creating a communication protocol for VLMs comes with interesting problems due to the density of information offered by the vision modality.
